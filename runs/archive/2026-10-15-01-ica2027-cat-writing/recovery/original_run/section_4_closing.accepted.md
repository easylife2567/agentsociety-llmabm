## English draft

Under shared external input and a fixed population, the curation regimes generated divergent output trajectories. Chronological and interest-based curation showed concentrated mourning-type output followed by late growth and majority shares for memetic-type authors, whereas late output from that group remained limited under random curation. Process records across regimes, together with component ablations under interest-based curation, connected personal exposure, participation and the recirculation of generated posts, showing how changes in participation timing and persistence reshaped subsequent exposure and supply composition. Together with the platform observations in Chapter 3, these model findings provide a basis for discussing how algorithmic curation and user expression jointly organize posthumous representation.
