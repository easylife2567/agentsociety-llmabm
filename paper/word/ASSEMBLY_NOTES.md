Public discussion after a public figure dies can alter how that person is represented over time. This study combines content analysis of 49,930 Douyin and Xiaohongshu posts about Zhang Xuefeng with agent simulations using a large language model. Observed posts show concentrated mourning followed by growth in memetic expression, with different late compositions across platforms. Nine simulation runs compare random, chronological, and interest curation under shared inputs and fixed author groups, while nine component ablations examine the expression rule. Chronological and interest curation produce late supply dominated by memetic authors, whereas random curation yields limited supply from this group. Process records and ablations connect individual exposure, selective participation, and recirculation, showing how participation timing and persistence reorganize supply and visibility. The findings advance an account of postmortem representation as a changing distribution of public voices and provide a perspective on the mechanisms linking curation and expression.
